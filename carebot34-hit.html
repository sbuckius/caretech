✦ Critical Context · CAREBOT-34
⚠️ The demographics of tech power have barely changed in 50 years.
Women held 10.4% of Fortune 500 CEO positions in 2024 — rising slightly to 11% in 2025.[1] In tech specifically, the picture is starker: as of Safra Catz's departure from Oracle in September 2025, all 10 of the largest US tech companies by market cap are led by men — 6 of whom are white, 3 South Asian, and 1 Taiwanese-American.[2] No Black woman has ever been CEO of a top-10 tech company. The Forbes Billionaires list is over 90% male, and the median tech billionaire is a white American man in his 50s who founded or co-founded his company.[3] Corporate boards have made more visible progress than the C-suite: women now hold roughly 33% of S&P 500 board seats, up from about 25% five years earlier.[4] But the pipeline underneath that progress is still uneven: for every 100 men promoted to manager, only 72 women are.[5] The chair has a demographic profile.
