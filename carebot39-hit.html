✦ CAREBOT-39 · The Irony
Computing and engineering use the word "mother" to mean the most essential, generative, originating component in any system. The sector that encodes "mother" as its highest technical authority is the sector that has most aggressively excluded mothers from its workforce.
Motherboard. Mother chip. Mother die. Mother clock. Mother copy. Mother process. Mother mold. Mother gauge. Mother pipe. Mother vessel. Mother disc. Rope Mother. Mother of All Demos.

In every case: mother = origin, source, generative authority, the controlling center from which everything derives. First attested use of "motherboard" in Electronics magazine, 1965. The first actual motherboard — the Planar Breadboard — designed by IBM engineer Patty McHugh, 1981. The "Rope Mother" who oversaw the software that landed on the moon: Margaret Hamilton, who coined the term "software engineering." The women who wove the core ropes she designed were called "little old ladies." They had previously worked at textile mills.

The numbers: Women hold 22% of global AI roles. 12% of AI researchers are women. 455,000 women left the US workforce in the first 8 months of 2025. The technology that calls its central component "mother" has built no infrastructure to keep mothers employed. The word is everywhere in computing. The mothers are being pushed out.
✦ The Full Taxonomy — "Mother" in Computing and Engineering
Across hardware, software, manufacturing, and systems: "mother" always means the same thing. The origin. The source. The thing that everything else depends on.
Hardware · 1965
Motherboard
// main printed circuit board; connects CPU, RAM, storage, all peripherals
First attested in Electronics magazine, 1965. "The mother of all boards" — from which all other components depend. The first true motherboard (Planar Breadboard, 1981) was designed by IBM engineer Patty McHugh. Her name does not appear in the Wikipedia article. Her board became the standard for every PC since.
Source: Wikipedia Motherboard · Britannica
Hardware · Chip Architecture
Mother Chip / Mother Die
// primary chip that controls subsidiary chips; primary die interconnecting chiplets
The mother chip is the master chip — everything else is subordinate. The mother die (Intel/AMD chiplet packaging) is the central die from which all other chiplets receive their instructions. The chip architecture that runs modern AI is organized around the concept of maternal authority — even as women hold 12% of AI research positions.
Hardware · Timing
Mother Clock
// master clock signal that drives and synchronizes all subsystems
The mother clock generates the fundamental rhythm of the entire computer. Without it, nothing synchronizes. Everything else follows the mother clock's timing. The metaphor: a mother sets the pace for everything in the household. The computing use is precise: the clock that governs all others.
Software / Unix · 1969+
Mother Process
// parent process that spawns child processes in Unix/Linux
In Unix/Linux (and every operating system since), processes run in a parent-child hierarchy. The mother process spawns child processes that inherit its properties and permissions. Every program running on every device uses this architecture. The mother process is the originating authority. Kill the mother process and the children die. The metaphor runs the internet.
Data / Archiving
Mother Copy
// original authoritative version from which all copies are made
The mother copy (also "master copy") is the original file, disk image, or codebase from which every other version is derived. Corrupt the mother copy and all derivative copies are compromised. The mother copy is the source of truth. It is the thing that the copies try to reproduce and never fully match.
Networking
Mother Server / Mother Node
// origin server feeding secondary/mirror servers; parent node in tree structures
The mother server is the primary node — it feeds data to mirror and secondary servers that replicate and distribute it. The mother node in any tree data structure is the parent relative to all children below it. The entire internet's DNS system, content delivery networks, and distributed databases run on this parent-child model.
Machining / Manufacturing
Mother Jig · Mother Gauge · Mother Mold · Mother Tool
// reference standards from which all working copies are derived
The mother jig is the master jig used to produce identical daughter jigs on factory floors. The mother gauge is the reference gauge used to calibrate all others. The mother mold is the rigid outer shell that supports casting. The mother tool is the original precision tool from which all derivative tools are made. In manufacturing: mother = the calibration standard everything else must match.
Vinyl / Optical Media
Mother Disc
// intermediate stamper from the master recording used to press all copies
In vinyl record production, the mother disc (or lacquer) is the intermediate stamper created from the original master recording. From the mother disc, all stampers are made. From the stampers, all copies are pressed. The mother disc is the origin of every record that ever played. It is usually destroyed in the process.
Metallurgy
Mother Alloy · Mother Ingot · Mother Metal
// base alloy / ingot from which all derivative metals are created
The mother alloy is the base metal from which all working alloys are derived by dilution, combination, and heat treatment. The mother ingot is the primary cast form from which all billets and shapes are cut. In metallurgy: every object made of metal descends from a mother. Every alloy has a maternal origin.
Hydraulics / Offshore
Mother Pipe · Mother Vessel
// main supply pipe / primary ship from which everything branches or deploys
The mother pipe is the main supply line from which all branch pipes are tapped in hydraulic, pneumatic, and plumbing systems. The mother vessel in offshore engineering is the primary ship that deploys smaller vessels, ROVs, and support craft. In fluid systems and maritime engineering: mother = the origin from which all flow derives.
✦ The Phrase · December 9, 1968
Mother of All Demos
// Douglas Engelbart's 90-minute demonstration at Brooks Hall, San Francisco — "the mother of all demos"
On December 9, 1968, Douglas Engelbart and his SRI team gave a 90-minute demonstration to 1,000 computer scientists at Brooks Hall, San Francisco. In 90 minutes, he introduced: the computer mouse, word processing, hypertext, shared-screen collaboration, multiple windows, video teleconferencing, and real-time collaborative editing. None of these had been publicly demonstrated before. The audience gave a standing ovation. The demo was retroactively named "the Mother of All Demos" because it generated everything that came after. Mouse. Internet. Collaboration tools. Windows. All of it was born there. The mother of modern computing.
Source: Smithsonian / Lemelson Center · Wikipedia
✦ The Women at the Origin
Margaret Hamilton · Rope Mother · 1965–1972
She coined "software engineering." She went to the moon.
Margaret Hamilton was lead software engineer for the Apollo missions at MIT. She coined the term "software engineering" and developed the foundational concepts of asynchronous software and priority-driven task execution that underpin all reliable software today. She was nicknamed the "Rope Mother" because she oversaw the core rope memory system that stored the Apollo Guidance Computer programs: copper wires threaded through magnetic cores, encoding ones and zeros. The women who wove those ropes had previously worked in textile mills. Hamilton called them "LOLs" — "little old ladies." Their manual dexterity, developed in textile labor, became the hands that wrote the code that landed on the moon.

Hamilton took her daughter Lauren to work at night and on weekends — she was a working mother with no other childcare option. Her daughter once crashed the simulator by "playing astronaut." This led Hamilton to build error-detection routines that later saved the Apollo 8 mission from abort. The mother brought the child. The child broke the machine. The mother fixed the machine. The machine went to the moon.

She received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016. She was 80 years old.
Patty McHugh · IBM Engineer · 1981
She designed the first motherboard.
Patty McHugh, an IBM engineer, designed the Planar Breadboard in 1981 — the first true motherboard, used in the IBM Personal Computer. Her design: a 4.77MHz Intel microprocessor, 16KB of memory, 8-bit ISA connectors, keyboard and cassette ports. Her blueprint became the standard for every personal computer motherboard that followed. Britannica credits her. Wikipedia's Motherboard article does not name her. The "mother of all boards" was designed by a woman whose name was not in the canonical history.
