The Eight Pillars · EMBRACE
1
The full HUMAN arc Humans are not machines. Humans build machines. We center every person in the chain: those who design the machines, build them, train them, use them, and are left to live with what they leave behind. Technology does not exist outside of human hands and human consequences — and a caring technological future accounts for every person in that chain, not just the ones at the top of it.
2
CARE-centered Humans CARE. Machines do not CARE. Humans must design technology from a perspective of human empathy, consideration, and community by including the voices of diverse people and centering human wellbeing across the full spectrum of human experience.
3
Challenging bias Technology is not neutral. We must name and dismantle the structural inequities baked into technological systems, datasets, and design choices.
4
Underamplified histories Not all stories are amplified and shared. We uncover and share the contributions of those whose work has been erased, credited to others, or never recorded at all.
5
Equitable futures Agency, Power, and Access. We must build a technology landscape where diverse voices don't just contribute, but shape the direction — one carebot at a time.
6
Caring for the world Technology impacts the enivornment and world. We must ask what technologies we owe the planet and which technologies harm it.
7
Human wisdom Humans have many types of complex knowledge. Machines can compute, but do not possess values, ethics, morals, feelings, common sense, context, understanding, opinions, awareness, passion, experience, empathy, CARE. When working with machines, humans should retain the authority, decision-making power, vision, and imagination. Machines do not posses the wisdom of humans. Human knowledge lives in hands, in judgment, in care. We must work to understand what must never be handed to machines and to ensure what is automated augments people so that they can do more of what only humans can: listening, grieving, connecting, deciding together.
8
Playful, absurd resistanceAbsurdity can reveal the irony, challenges, concerns, and power structures in systems. Absurdity emerges when there is a disconnect between the way that things are and the way that we might hope they are. Absurdity and playfulness can also be a form of whimsical resistance. We hope to challenge the dominant paradigms of technoptimism, technological determinism, technocapitalism, and inevitable march of technological progress. When we encounter these systems with these strucutres and it does not suit our goals, absurdity emerges. When we expect humans to be like technology, and they aren't, absurdity takes over.
We are not adding diversity to technology. We are restoring what was always there — and accounting for every human hand it passed through to get here.

A Note on AI (Automation)

Sarah used AI (automation) to help construct the HTML to set up this website by prompting it with details about the idea, the features, the parts, and the framework. Sarah believes that many things should not be automated using AI and that users should be cautious about the inaccuracies and bias in the system, exploitation of workers, the environmental impact, and the unethical use of the work of people without compensation, consent, or attribution.

Another issue that might negatively impact humans is the process by which it is forced into our lives and technology and work — and thus could result in our becoming reliant on it, without the ability to back out. Once everyone must use it, it forces the speed and style and aesthetics into the system, which requires everyone to utilize it to stay relevant and up to speed. This is a concern, especially because the design, deployment, and use is in the power of a few individuals (without guardrails or oversight). Sarah worries about this reliance on the technology when those in power make the cost to use it high — and this creates an equity issue.

While Sarah still struggles with the issues related to how AI (automation) could impact humans and the world, Sarah is experimenting with using it to support education and equity in instances where it could support students and learning. The use of it in this case is debatable, because I used it to help me generate the code for the website that I envisioned. Even though it is close to what I had in my mind, it is very highly possible that merely using it created the images in my mind. This process might result in more and more technology looking more dull and the same. Perhaps this website will end up being a relic of this strange time — a speculative capsule stuck in a weird, slopification automation void.
